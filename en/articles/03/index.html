Dog and Cat Vaccination Schedule | When to Administer the 5-in-1, 8-in-1, and Rabies Vaccines?

A comprehensive guide on when to start vaccinating your puppy or kitten, protecting them from dangerous viral diseases, and how to prepare.

If you have a puppy or a kitten, the first question that probably comes to mind is: "When should I start vaccinations? And at what age are the 5-in-1, 8-in-1, and Rabies vaccines administered?"

Vaccinations are the most crucial step in preventative care, protecting your pet from dangerous viral diseases that could threaten their life, while preparing their immune system to fight off infections.

In this article, you will learn about the vaccination schedule for dogs and cats, the timings for the 5-in-1, 8-in-1, and Rabies vaccines, along with essential tips before and after vaccination.

Why are vaccinations important?

From the very first weeks of a puppy or kitten's life, their body relies on the maternal immunity they received. However, over time, this immunity begins to fade, leaving the animal vulnerable to severe diseases. This is where vaccinations come in. They train the immune system to recognize viruses and bacteria, enabling it to fight them off quickly if exposed in the future.

Dog Vaccination Schedule 🐶

Most veterinarians start the vaccination program from approximately 45 days of age.

The 5-in-1 or 8-in-1 Vaccine

These are the most important vaccines for puppies, protecting them against a range of the most dangerous viral diseases, such as:

  • Parvovirus
  • Distemper
  • Viral Hepatitis
  • Parainfluenza

The 8-in-1 vaccine may include additional protection against other diseases like Leptospirosis, depending on the vaccine brand used.

Puppy Age Vaccination
45 days (approx. 1.5 months) 1st Dose
After 21 days 2nd Dose
After 21 days 3rd Dose

Note: Dose timings or numbers may vary slightly depending on the puppy's age at the start of vaccination and the type of vaccine used. The veterinarian will determine the most appropriate schedule for each case.

Rabies Vaccine

Usually given at 3 months of age, or according to local regulations and veterinary recommendations. Subsequent booster doses are given depending on the vaccine type.

Cat Vaccination Schedule 🐱

The vaccination program generally begins between 45 and 60 days of age.

The 3-in-1 or 4-in-1 Vaccine (FVRCP)

Protects cats from major viral diseases such as:

  • Feline Panleukopenia (Feline Parvovirus).
  • Feline Herpesvirus.
  • Feline Calicivirus.
Kitten Age Vaccination
45–60 days 1st Dose
After 21 days 2nd Dose
After 21 days 3rd Dose (if recommended by the vet)

Rabies Vaccine for Cats

Usually given at 3 months of age, followed by booster doses determined by the vaccine type and local regulations.

Booster Doses

After completing the first year of the animal's life, the booster program begins to maintain immunity. In most cases:

  • A booster dose one year after the last vaccination.
  • The veterinarian then determines the schedule for subsequent doses based on the vaccine type, the animal's age, and lifestyle.

Before Vaccination... Do These Things

For the best response to the vaccine, your pet must be:

  • ✅ In good health.
  • ✅ Free from fever.
  • ✅ Without diarrhea or vomiting.
  • ✅ Free from worms and parasites as much as possible.

Therefore, veterinarians often recommend administering a deworming dose before starting the vaccination program if due.

After Vaccination... What is Normal?

After vaccination, you might notice:

  • Mild lethargy.
  • Sleeping more than usual.
  • Decreased appetite for a day or two.
  • Mild pain at the injection site.

This is normal in most cases. However, if the following occurs: severe facial swelling, difficulty breathing, repeated vomiting, or fainting, you must head to the veterinarian immediately.

Does Vaccination Protect Against Fleas and Ticks?

Answer: No. Vaccinations protect against viral and bacterial diseases but do not prevent external parasites like fleas and ticks. Therefore, veterinarians recommend using dedicated flea and tick control products as part of the preventative care program, especially during the summer.

Common Questions

When is the first vaccination for a puppy?

Usually at 45 days to 8 weeks of age, based on the veterinarian's assessment.

How many times is the 5-in-1 given?

Typically three doses, separated by about 21 days, but the program may vary based on the puppy's age at the start and the vaccine type.

Is the 8-in-1 better than the 5-in-1?

Both provide protection against major viral diseases. The 8-in-1 may include extra protection depending on the vaccine. The vet determines the best fit for each case.

Is a deworming dose necessary before vaccination?

In many cases, yes, because the presence of worms can affect overall health, so the vet determines the appropriate timing for deworming before starting vaccinations.

What if I missed a vaccination date?

Do not restart the program on your own. Consult the veterinarian to determine the best way to resume the schedule without losing protection.

Conclusion

Committing to the vaccination schedule for dogs and cats is the first step in protecting your pet from severe diseases, but it is not the only step. Daily care, proper nutrition, flea and tick control, and regular veterinary check-ups all complement each other to help your pet live a healthy and safe life.
